# Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) is the process of analyzing, visualizing, and understanding your data before building any machine learning model.  
Think of EDA as detective work — you're looking at your data with curiosity before building models or doing transformations.

---

## 🔑 Importance of EDA
- You can't clean or preprocess what you don't understand.
- Helps identify mistakes, biases, or limitations in the data.
- Guides data cleaning and feature engineering.
- Provides a "story" or overview of what the data is telling you.

---

## 🛠️ Steps in EDA

### 1. Viewing the Data
- Use functions like `head()`, `tail()`, `shape`, `info()`
- Questions to ask:
  - What columns do I have?
  - What types of data are present?

---

### 2. Summary Statistics
- Calculate mean, median, mode, standard deviation, min, max, quartiles.
- Helps understand spread and central tendency.

---

### 3. Value Counts
- Count unique values in a column.
- Useful for categorical columns.

---

### 4. Missing Value Analysis
- Identify gaps in the dataset.
- Calculate percentage of missing data.

---

### 5. Visualizations
- **Histograms** → distribution of values  
- **Boxplots** → outliers and spread  
- **Bar plots** → comparisons of categories  
- **Correlation heatmaps** → linear relationships between numerical features  
- **Scatter plots** → bivariate relationships  

---

### 6. Target Variable Exploration
- Explore how the output variable relates to other features.
- Example: How does `charges` vary with age, BMI, or region?

---

## 🚀 Summary
EDA tells you **what’s wrong** with the data.  
Data Cleaning fixes it.  
Although not glamorous, EDA and cleaning are **80% of the work** in real-world ML projects.
